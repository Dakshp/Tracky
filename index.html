Sync to a Google Sheet

Without this, expenses live only in this browser on this phone. Connect a Google Sheet and they are stored there instead — shared across your devices, readable as a normal spreadsheet, and reachable by a shortcut that never has to open the app. Setup instructions are in server/README.md in the project. The address below is not the sheet’s own link — it is the one Apps Script gives you after Deploy → New deployment → Web app, ending in /exec.

The shortcut token can only add expenses — it cannot read or change anything. Set a different random string for it in the script, since a shortcut has to carry its token in a URL. Leave blank to use the main token instead.

Quick add shortcut

Build it in the Shortcuts app:

  1. Ask for Input — Number — “How much did you spend?”
  2. Choose from Menu — one item per category, named exactly as in the list above (Food & Drink, Groceries, …)
  3. Ask for Input — Text — “Note (optional)”
  4. Text — paste the address above, replacing AMOUNT, CATEGORY and NOTE with those three answers

Then in iOS Settings assign it to Back Tap or the Lock Screen, or say “Hey Siri, shortcut name”.

Bring data from another app

Most expense apps can export a CSV file. Pick one here and Tracky will read it, show you what it found, and let you check it before anything is saved. Your existing expenses are kept — this adds to them.

Your data

Everything is stored on this device only. Back up before switching phones or clearing your browser.

About

Tracky is a daily expense tracker — add a spend in a couple of taps, and see where the month is going.

  • Private by default. Every expense stays in your phone’s own storage — offline, instant, nothing sent unless you say so.
  • Your sheet, not our server. Sync writes to a Google Sheet in your account. Nothing sits in between.
  • Add one without opening it. A Shortcut or a word to Siri is enough — Tracky has a small API of its own.

On iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen.
